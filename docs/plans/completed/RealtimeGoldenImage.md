# Realtime Golden Image — A Reference The Specification Determines

Drafted 2026-09-12 against `0662778` and closed the same day at `a4274ba`. Five steps across
`api/render/tests` and the documents, taking up what [RenderTestsInCI](RenderTestsInCI.md) left
when it closed: the device half of `api/render/realtime` now draws in CI, and what it asserted
about the picture was five texels checked by hand.

**Closed with four pictures pinned** - a flat quad, a quad drawn with an uploaded texture, and
two overlapping world quads in each submission order - each byte-exact on a Radeon RX 6600 and
on the runner's Mesa llvmpipe 26.2.0. That is the claim ADR-0007's fifth alternative said could
not be had, measured rather than argued.

## Why now

[ADR-0007](../../adr/0007-ci-rendering-tests.md) chose validation silence over pixels and deferred
golden images in its fifth alternative rather than rejecting them.
[ADR-0050](../../adr/0050-a-frame-is-read-back-in-two-calls.md) built the half that was missing and
said outright that ADR-0007 stands until something blesses a reference. Nothing has.

What that leaves uncovered is named in [TODO.md](../../TODO.md): the renderers themselves, the
pipeline cache and the upload path are asserted by nothing that draws. `v3dtest_render_device`
draws a clear and a quad, so what it establishes is that a frame can be recorded, submitted and
read back at all. A quad drawn in the wrong colour, at the wrong scale, or with the wrong
texture reaches the same five texels and passes four of them.

The other half of the argument is [sdlc.md](../../sdlc.md)'s: *"a rendering change is verified by
running the app and reading the log"*. That is a person, per change, for the newest code in the
tree.

## The obstacle, and what answers it

ADR-0007's objection to golden images is the whole of the design problem, and it is correct as
stated: *"Reference images must be generated by the same software rasterizer to be stable, which
makes them useless as a check of hardware behaviour."* A picture blessed on this machine's
Radeon and compared against lavapipe on the runner fails on the difference between two
conformant implementations, and a picture blessed on lavapipe asserts nothing about the driver
an app actually runs on.

The answer is to bless only pictures the specification determines — axis aligned geometry on
integer pixel boundaries, flat colours at the ends of the channel range, nearest sampling, no
blending and no multisampling — so that every conformant implementation owes the same bytes and
the file stops being any one rasterizer's. What may be in a reference, and what a case that
wants more does instead, is [ADR-0054](../../adr/0054-a-realtime-reference-is-a-picture-the-spec-determines.md).

**This is also what the ordering below is built around.** The claim is not provable on this
machine: there is one gpu here, and the runner's lavapipe is the second implementation. So the
first committed picture is a probe — the cheapest one that could disagree — and it goes through
CI before any other is blessed.

### What blocks what

Step 1 blocks 3, 4 and 5 and not 2: the seam is the same mechanism whatever the rule turns out
to be, and the rule is what decides which pictures are allowed through it.

Step 3 was the barrier, and it is closed: the rule is measured across two implementations rather
than argued. **Steps 4 and 5 are independent of each other and neither waits on anything now**,
though each is a new picture and so a new claim under the same rule.

### What this does not take up

- **Text.** `ui::TextRenderer` is uncovered for a reason that outlives this plan — it needs a
  window — and a signed distance field glyph is antialiased, so its edge texels are exactly what
  ADR-0054 refuses to pin.
- **The pipeline cache.** It is named beside the renderers in TODO.md as unasserted, and what
  would assert it is a count of what was compiled rather than a picture. That is a case in the
  device suite either way, and it is not a golden image.
- **Blending, filtering and multisampling.** Not deferred for effort: a picture of any of them
  is a picture of one implementation, which is the thing this plan exists not to commit.

## Step 1 — What a reference is

ADR-0054, written before the first picture is blessed rather than after, because it is the rule
that decides which cases can have one at all.

## Step 2 — The comparison seam

`api/render/tests/device/Reference.h` — read the committed png, compare it against what the
capture wrote, and write what was rendered to `data_out/` when they differ or when the reference
is absent. The same shape `moya/tests/ReferenceTest.cxx` and talyn's already use, so a failure
reports the worst pixel and where it was through `image::compare`.

Two differences from the offline suites, both from ADR-0054. The tolerance is zero rather than
one, because the offline renderers' tolerance is for float rounding across compilers and a
realtime reference is either determined or is not admissible. And the comparison is against the
png the capture wrote rather than against its buffer, which the offscreen cases already do.

## Step 3 — The probe

`a_drawn_quad_is_silent` checks five texels. It becomes a case that checks a picture, against
`data/quad.png`, and the five checks go: a reference that covers every texel subsumes them.

This is the step that measures the rule. It is deliberately the dullest picture the suite can
draw — one flat red rect on black, at integer boundaries — so that a disagreement between this
machine and lavapipe is a disagreement about rasterization itself rather than about anything
this plan chose.

**State — the rule holds.** The picture was blessed on a Radeon RX 6600 and compares clean on
Mesa llvmpipe 26.2.0, exactly, on the first run that reached it. It holds two colours and no
others, every channel of each is 0 or 255, and the red covers exactly x over [16, 47] and y over
[8, 23] — no partially covered texel exists in it for two implementations to disagree about,
which is the whole of what ADR-0054 asks of a reference.

That run did fail, and on something else: the workflow step that re-runs the suite to turn a
skip into a failure ran the executable from the workspace root rather than from beside itself,
so `data/quad.png` resolved to nothing. Nothing in that suite had read a file before, so no
previous run could have caught it. ctest, which sets the working directory the way
`v3d_add_test` asks, passed the case in the same job.

## Step 4 — The upload path

A quad drawn with a texture the case uploads, sampled nearest at one texel per pixel. Nothing in
the tree asserts `vulkan::Uploader` at all today, and a texture that arrived transposed, in the
wrong channel order, or in the wrong mip is a picture rather than a validation error.

The texture is generated in the case rather than committed beside the reference: a file would be
a second thing to keep in step with the picture, and an asymmetric pattern built in four lines
says the same thing about orientation.

**State.** Landed. Four quadrants of full range colour over a 32 by 16 texture, drawn at one
texel per pixel. The picture holds five colours and no others and every channel of each is 0 or
255, which is the measurement that matters: the tree's sampler is `VK_FILTER_LINEAR` and has no
nearest to ask for, and at one texel per pixel it returned every texel unchanged with no blended
value at any quadrant seam. ADR-0054 was amended to say that in terms of what the sampler
returns rather than which filter it is.

## Step 5 — Depth and order

Two opaque world quads that overlap, drawn near first and then far first, per
[ADR-0042](../../adr/0042-a-textured-quad-in-world-space.md). This is the only case here that draws
through `renderer::World` or puts a depth attachment on a render target, so it is what says
either works at all.

**State. Landed, and the step as drafted asserted the opposite of the ADR it cites.** The text
above said the picture should be the same both times "or the depth test is not doing what the
ADR says it does". ADR-0042 says the caller supplies the order: the depth tested pipeline
`depth(true, false)` tests and does not write, so solid geometry occludes a quad and one quad
never occludes another. The drafted assertion would have failed against a renderer that is
correct, and it did — the first run of it put the far quad over the near one, which is the
specified behaviour.

What landed pins that instead: a picture per submission order, and a direct assertion that the
two differ. A pipeline that wrote depth would make them one picture, which is the regression the
case now fails on. The other half of ADR-0042 — the solid geometry that does occlude a quad — is
not drawn, because nothing in this tree writes depth; it needs a consumer's own pipeline.
